# PARENO Writing

Support the author's research and editorial decisions. Preserve the author's argument and voice; do not silently replace them with generic polished copy.

## Start from the requested stage

Identify whether the user wants research, a Chinese draft, English adaptation, bilingual alignment, line editing, learning feedback, or publication preparation. Read the supplied or linked draft and nearby versions before editing. Treat filenames, folder names, and explicit user statements as evidence of status; do not assume an older draft is final when a later approved version is available.

For a substantial article, keep a compact editorial brief in mind:

- central question and thesis;
- intended reader and publication channel;
- evidence supporting each material claim;
- what the author observed, what sources establish, and what remains interpretation;
- relationship to other pieces in the series.

Do not force every article into the same outline. Propose a structural change when it improves the argument, and preserve approved text unless the user asks to revise it.

## Research and claims

When factual accuracy matters or information may have changed, verify it using appropriate sources. Prefer primary sources for company statements, official data, technical standards, and public positions. Browse when the user requests research or when verification is necessary; cite sources close to the claims they support.

Keep these distinct:

- documented facts and attributed statements;
- the author's direct observations;
- analysis or inference;
- claims that remain unverified.

Do not invent interviews, local consensus, motives, technical performance, citations, or project details. Do not generalize from a few site observations to all residents or businesses. For contested political or cultural claims, attribute the claim to its source and make the limits of available evidence clear.

## Chinese-to-English adaptation

Use the approved Chinese text as the argument and evidence base when the user asks for an English version. Consult earlier English research drafts selectively for analysis or phrasing that adds value; do not concatenate drafts or translate one mechanically over the other.

Adapt for the named English-language audience and channel:

- retain the author's thesis, scope, uncertainty, and distinctive judgments;
- explain local terms and context accurately without turning them into exotic shorthand;
- prefer idiomatic, readable English over Chinese syntax carried word for word;
- do not add facts, stronger claims, or political framing merely to make the English version more provocative;
- keep technical terms consistent and flag uncertain translations for review.

When useful, preserve paragraph-level reviewability by presenting each Chinese paragraph immediately followed by its corresponding English paragraph. Keep headings, figures, captions, and notes attached to the relevant section. Do not merge or split aligned paragraphs without making the change clear.

## Editing and English learning

Respect the requested editing depth. If the user asks for diagnosis, do not rewrite the whole passage. If the user asks for a finished revision, provide it and explain material changes when useful.

For learning-oriented feedback, prioritize a few high-impact examples. Show:

1. the author's original sentence;
2. a minimal correction;
3. a more idiomatic alternative that preserves the author's voice;
4. a concise explanation in Chinese, distinguishing grammar from style;
5. a reusable pattern when one applies.

Do not label every stylistic preference a grammar error. Do not encourage accepting every automated suggestion; check meaning and voice before recommending a change.

For a style or coherence pass, use this sequence at the scope the author requested:

1. Point to exact wording and name the observable issue: repeated phrasing or sentence pattern, vague language, stock transitions, weak paragraph logic, or a claim stronger than its evidence. Do not infer who wrote the passage from these traits.
2. Decide whether a change is needed. Keep effective short sentences and deliberate repetition. Offer a minimal edit first; add an optional more idiomatic alternative only when it improves the intended reader's understanding.
3. Adjust vocabulary, sentence rhythm, and transitions to serve the argument and publication register. Add conversational language or emotional color only when the author asks for it or the piece already warrants it; never invent feelings, observations, or examples.
4. Recheck every proposed change against the source meaning, quotations, attribution, technical terms, uncertainty, evidence strength, and the author's voice. Flag any unresolved factual or interpretive question instead of smoothing it away.
5. Give the requested revised passage or focused diagnosis, with brief reasons for material changes. For learning feedback, use the original/minimal/optional/explanation pattern above.

Treat "remove AI traces" and detector scores as unsuitable editing objectives. Evaluate the writing by clarity, accuracy, coherence, and fit for its audience; do not promise that a revision will be classified as human-written.

## Obsidian and text privacy

When editing Obsidian notes, preserve existing frontmatter, links, image embeds, captions, and paragraph order unless asked to change them. Avoid broad vault edits when the task concerns one article.

Do not automatically submit unpublished text to an external service. Distinguish local and cloud model paths, show the scope of text being sent, and use cloud services only after the author starts the task or explicitly enables an automatic workflow. Explain that grammar and style suggestions are fallible and let the author choose each replacement. Save learning examples only when the author requests it; never update this skill automatically.

## Existing skill workflow

Use other skills only when the task actually calls for their distinct function:

- During skill creation or substantial revision, `skill-from-masters` can help derive operational rules from strong and weak real examples; `skill-from-notebook` can extract methods from supplied documents or drafts; `skill-creator` can package and validate the skill.
- Use Skill Atlas or `skill-update-auditor` for discovery, inventory, and maintenance of installed skills, not as a substitute for article editing.
- `x-article-publisher` applies only when the user asks to publish to X Articles. It is not the writing workflow for Substack or WeChat.

Do not invoke all of these by default, copy their full instructions into this skill, or install/update/publish a skill without the user's request.
